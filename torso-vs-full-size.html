Decision guide Weight · Drying · Storage

Choose a torso or full-size doll by the routine, not the catalog photo.

The useful dividing line is not “realistic” versus “compact.” It is how much product you can move, rinse, dry, support and store without turning the ownership routine into a burden.

Published and reviewed November 25, 2026 · Current examples from Quzio's public catalog

Short answer

Choose the torso with the easier routine.

A silicone torso is usually the better first choice when one person handles the product, dries it in a bathroom or compact room, or needs to put it away after use. The main compromise is less complete-body presence and posing.

Short answer

Choose the full-size doll when the body matters.

A full-size companion is for buyers who value a complete figure, face and articulated pose, and who can plan the delivery route, wet handling, drying area and long-term storage before checkout.

Measure these five things before you decide

  1. Delivery route

    Check doorway width, stairs, elevator space and a safe place to open the outer box. Keep the route clear before delivery day.

  2. Lifting plan

    Decide where the product will be lifted from and placed down. A 39.6 lb torso can be difficult when wet even if it is manageable when dry.

  3. Drying footprint

    You need a stable surface where every internal area can dry and where water can drain or be contained. A sealed storage bin is not a drying area.

  4. Storage height and depth

    Do not measure standing height alone. Account for the folded or supported position, the cover, the accessory box and access to the storage location.

  5. Your real maintenance window

    Estimate the time you will consistently spend rinsing, drying, inspecting and storing the product. Choose a model that fits the routine you will actually repeat.

Which one should a first-time buyer choose?

Start with a torso if…

  • You live alone in a compact space and must put it away after use.
  • You want to handle and dry the product without a second person.
  • You care more about manageable ownership than complete-body presence.
  • A 17–29 lb model fits your physical handling limits better than a large torso.

Start with a full-size doll if…

  • You have dedicated storage and a supported drying area.
  • Complete-body posing, face and overall presence are central to the purchase.
  • You can plan delivery and moving without rushing.
  • You accept a longer care and handling routine.

Where owner experience changes the answer

Weight is easier to understand when owners describe the full routine: moving the product while wet, reaching every drying area, finding a stable surface and returning it to storage. A product that feels manageable in a showroom or unboxing can still be difficult to own if the bathroom and storage layout do not support it.

FAQ

Is a torso always easier to clean than a full-size doll?

No. A torso usually has less surface area and less mass, but a 39.6 lb large torso can still be difficult to move and dry. Compare the specific model, surface material, weight and routine.

Is 39.6 lb too heavy for one person?

That depends on the person, the grip, the surface and whether the product is wet. Do not lift beyond your comfortable limit. Use a planned route, a stable support and help if needed.

Do silicone torsos need drying?

Yes. Internal areas should be cleaned and dried completely after use. A non-porous surface can be easier to rinse, but it does not remove the need for a repeatable drying routine.

Can I store either type in a sealed plastic bag?

Use a breathable cover and a suitable support surface. Avoid sealed storage and heat. Follow the manufacturer's current care guidance for the specific material and product.
